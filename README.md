# The lonely runner conjecture for eight and nine runners: a kernel-checked proof

Version 1.0.0 (19 November 2026). Author: Vico Bonfioli. License: CC-BY-4.0 (`LICENSE.md`).
Repository: https://github.com/ElVec1o/lonely-runner-eight-and-nine-runners
Paper: `paper/main.pdf` (21 pages; source `paper/main.tex`). Not peer reviewed.

## What this is

For positive integer speeds `v_1,...,v_k` let `ML(v) = max_t min_i dist(t v_i, Z)`, where `dist` is the distance to the nearest integer (the "maximum loneliness" of the lonely runner problem; `k` speeds are `k+1` runners, one of them standing still).
This repository contains a formalization in Lean 4 with Mathlib of the lonely runner conjecture for eight and for nine runners, together with a paper that explains the proofs.

> **Nine runners (eight speeds):** for every `v : Fin 8 -> Int` with all `v i > 0`, `1/9 <= ML v`.
> **Eight runners (seven speeds):** for every `v : Fin 7 -> Int` with all `v i > 0`, `1/8 <= ML v`.

The Lean theorems are
`LRSpectrumFull.NineSound.Plan9.Assemble9Final.nine_runners_unconditional : forall v : Fin 8 -> Int, (forall i, 0 < v i) -> 1/9 <= ML v` and
`LRSpectrumFull.EightRunnersReal.eight_runners_unconditional_real : forall v : Fin 7 -> Int, (forall i, 0 < v i) -> 1/8 <= ML v`.
**Neither has a hypothesis.** Both depend on exactly the three standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` (no `sorry`, no `native_decide`, no further axiom); the printouts from the final assembly are in `repro/expected_output/`.
The two cases are already known from computer-assisted proofs in the literature (see Prior art); **no new case of the conjecture is claimed**. The contribution is a formal proof of both statements in Lean, with every finite certificate checked by the Lean kernel; we make no claim of priority (see Prior art).

How the proof is organized (the paper is the authority):
* **Seven runners** (six speeds): `BarajasSerra.LRC6_barajasSerra`, a formalization of the case analysis of Barajas and Serra (about 20 modules `BarajasSerra*.lean`, built by lake).
* **Eight runners:** a reduction (`EightPlan`, `EightE5`, following Rosenfeld: the seven-runner case plus finite checks `lemma7Check 7 p` at the 27 primes `31, 37, ..., 163`) and, for each of the 27 primes, a finite certificate `lemma7Check 7 p = true` generated by a Rust program and checked by the Lean kernel (`decide +kernel`) in the staged modules `E8Stage.P<p>` (`repro/eight/`).
* **Nine runners:** the same scheme with 36 primes (`53, ..., 241`, `S36`) and cover trees at the moduli `p`, `3p`, `9p` (`NineSound/`, `repro/`); the rounding step uses the eight-runner theorem: `Assemble9Final.lrc7_of_eight` derives the statement `LRC7` (`1/8 <= ML u` for seven speeds) from `eight_runners_unconditional_real`, and `Assemble9.nine_runners_final : LRC7 -> ...` does the rest.

## Prior art

Seven runners: Barajas and Serra (Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 15, 2008). Eight runners: Rosenfeld (arXiv:2509.14111, preprint). Nine runners: Rosenfeld (arXiv:2512.01912, preprint) and Trakulthongchai (Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 33(2), 2026, #P2.46, up to ten runners). The references come from our literature notes of July 2026 and were not re-read for this release. Formalizations: H. Chen formalizes the conjecture in Lean for three to seven runners (`chlciallo/lean4-lonely-runner`, 2026). Our November 2026 searches for formalizations of the eight- and nine-runner statements did not find one, but they were not exhaustive and we make no claim of priority.

`ML v := sSup (Set.range (gap v))` with `gap v t := inf'_i nearestIntDist (v i * t)` and `nearestIntDist x := min (Int.fract x) (1 - Int.fract x)` (`LRSpectrumFull/MaxLoneliness.lean`, `NearestInteger.lean`). `repro/check_axioms.lean` prints these definitions, `LRC7` and `PrimeData`. Read them before trusting the English above.

## Verify

Needs `elan`, the Mathlib cache for the pinned revision (`lake exe cache get`; not done by the scripts), Rust (std only) and about 24 GB of memory. Run from the repository root.

```
cd repro
bash build.sh --plan                      # validate sources, hashes, pins, the per-prime build order; builds nothing (PLAN INCOMPLETE until the next step)
bash regen_data.sh --all --install        # regenerate the 7,849 omitted generated modules (632 MB) and compare sha256 with MANIFEST.tsv (about two minutes)
bash eight/stage/regen_e8stage.sh         # regenerate the 27 sets of staged eight-runner sources and compare sha256 with eight/E8STAGE.tsv (no Lean)
bash build.sh --run                       # the 25,985 lake-built modules, one at a time (about 42 h of summed module time for the 36 primes; not run in one piece)
bash eight/stage/run_prime.sh 31          # kernel check of one eight-runner prime (KEEP=1 keeps the oleans); 27 primes in all, hours for the large ones
bash assemble_final.sh --plan             # the three raw-lean assembly steps (needs the 36 per-prime archives and the 27 staged libraries); --run executes them
bash check_axioms.sh                      # print and judge the axioms: expect VERDICT: PASS (44 lines, 2 of them the planted-sorry controls)
```

`build.sh --plan` reports `PLAN INCOMPLETE` (exit 4) in the shipped state, because the omitted generated modules are missing; after `regen_data.sh --all --install` it reports `PLAN VALID`.
A quick check that needs no Lean: `shasum -a 256 -c CHECKSUMS.sha256 | grep -vc ': OK'` prints `0`.
Never run a bare `lake build` in `lean/` (one lean process of this project needs 4 to 10 GB, and lake starts one per core); the lakefile has no default target for this reason.

## What is checked, and by whom

* **The Lean kernel** checks the proofs, including every generated certificate (36 nine-runner primes, 27 eight-runner primes). `repro/check_axioms.sh` prints `#print axioms` for `nine_runners_unconditional`, `lrc7_of_eight`, `eight_runners_unconditional_real`, `LRC6_barajasSerra`, `nine_runners_final`, `pd36` and the 36 per-prime constants, and for two planted-`sorry` controls, and judges the output. **This script was prepared but not run on the shipped files**; the recorded printouts in `repro/expected_output/` come from the development tree (final assembly, three steps: `final_assembly_axioms.txt`; `assemble_raw.txt` for the nine-runner step; `per_prime_axioms_recorded.txt`).
* **Controls.** In the development tree, every nine-runner prime had a corrupted-table control and a planted-`sorry` control (`controls_ok`, `planted_sorry_detected` in `repro/results.tsv`); the final assembly was run with `LRC7 := sorry` (the printout lists `sorryAx`) and with three corrupted bridges (`1/7` instead of `1/8`, positivity dropped, the seven-speed theorem applied to eight speeds), all rejected. The control modules are not in the import closure and are not shipped.
* **The Rust generators are untrusted.** The kernel re-checks every generated table and certificate against Lean definitions whose soundness theorems are proved in Lean; a wrong generator can only make the build fail.
* **Regeneration.** `regen_data.sh --all` regenerates all 36 per-prime nine-runner trees and `eight/stage/regen_e8stage.sh` the 27 eight-runner source sets, and both compare every file with a recorded sha256; see `repro/README.md` and `repro/eight/README.md` for exactly what was compared.
* **Not checked by anyone**: that the Lean statement is the mathematical statement the reader has in mind (read the definitions above); `NineSound/DrvGSound.lean` and `gen_prime13` were not independently reviewed; the formalization of Barajas and Serra was not compared with the published proof by a second reader. No human has reviewed the proofs.

Versions: Lean 4.30.0 (`leanprover/lean4:v4.30.0`), Mathlib at revision `c5ea00351c28e24afc9f0f84379aa41082b1188f` (pinned in `lean/lake-manifest.json`).

## Honest limits

1. **The final assembly is one raw-lean elaboration over restored and staged libraries, not a from-scratch build.** `EightRunnersReal`, `Assemble9` and `Assemble9Final` were elaborated once each with the plain `lean` binary (127 s and 4.53 GB, 91 s and 5.57 GB, 112 s and 5.91 GB), against (a) the 36 nine-runner per-prime libraries restored from archives and (b) the 27 eight-runner per-prime libraries (949 `.olean` files) built outside lake in September 2026 and kept. Lake does not track these three modules: the archives contain no `ir/*.c` files, and the staged libraries live outside the lake package (`repro/assemble_final.sh`). The per-prime and staged libraries were **not recompiled in the final run**; their hashes (`repro/ARCHIVES.sha256`, `repro/eight/E8STAGE_OLEANS.sha256`) and the build records are shipped.
2. **No from-scratch rebuild in one go.** The 36 nine-runner libraries were built separately, over 17 to 19 November 2026, with restarts (several memory-limit failures and a change of generator from `gen_prime12` to `gen_prime13`, and stale sources from earlier attempts set aside before the final builds); summed module wall time of the final successful builds is about 42 hours (`repro/results.tsv`). The 27 eight-runner libraries were built in September 2026; the recorded wall times of the staged builds (two lean processes in parallel) add up to 38,567 s = 10.7 h (`repro/eight/E8STAGE_PRIMES.tsv`); the sources were deleted after each build and the oleans kept.
3. **Edited comments, and line 1 of the generated sources.** To make the repository readable, comments in the hand-written Lean modules were edited before release, and the first line (the "generated by" comment) of every generated module, of the generator sources and `mkfinal.sh`, and of the staged eight-runner sources was rewritten. The Lean declarations, tactics and terms (the kernel content) are unchanged (checked for the hand-written modules by comparing the sources with comments stripped, and for the generated modules by regenerating them and comparing with the development tree after normalizing line 1), but the shipped files are not byte-identical to the files from which the libraries were built; old and new sha256 of every edited hand-written file are in `repro/EDITED_COMMENTS.tsv`, and `MANIFEST.tsv`, `eight/E8STAGE.tsv` and `CHECKSUMS.sha256` carry the hashes of the files as shipped. The hashes in the eight-runner records name the original sources.
4. **Partial shipping.** 18,139 of the 25,988 modules of the import closure are shipped (119.3 MB). The 7,849 omitted modules (`D01`, `D2` and `TD*` of each nine-runner prime, 632.4 MB) are generated data, listed with sha256 in `repro/MANIFEST.tsv` and restored by `repro/regen_data.sh`. The staged eight-runner sources (949 files, 1577 MB) are not shipped: `repro/eight/E8STAGE.tsv` lists them with sha256 and `repro/eight/stage/regen_e8stage.sh` regenerates and compares them. The 36 nine-runner archives (641 MB) and the 949 staged eight-runner oleans (2.5 GB) are not shipped; their hashes are.
5. **The import closure contains library modules that are not on the proof path.** `EightCert27.lean` declares 27 axioms (`lemma7Check_seven_p<p>_verified`), which an earlier route used in place of the kernel checks; `EightMSS.lean`, `EightPlan.lean` (superseded stubs) and `BarajasSerra.lean` (an unfinished lemma) contain one `sorry` each in code; the proof of `LRC6_barajasSerra` does not use it. `EightE5` imports `EightCert27`, so the axioms are in the environment; `EightE5.eight_runners_unconditional` (the earlier route, named like the final theorem) depends on them, while `EightRunnersReal.eight_runners_unconditional_real` and the other headline theorems do not: `#print axioms` lists only the three standard axioms for the four headline theorems (recorded in `repro/expected_output/final_assembly_axioms.txt`). A `grep` for `axiom` or `sorry` over the sources therefore finds hits; the `#print axioms` output is the test that counts.
6. **Not claimed:** any new case of the lonely runner conjecture; the conjecture for all numbers of runners; repeated, zero, negative or non-integer speeds; shifted versions. The paper has not been peer reviewed.
7. **Provenance.** The Lean modules, Rust generators and the first draft of the paper were produced by an AI system (Claude, Anthropic); the questions, directions and the decision to formalize these statements came from Vico Bonfioli. The Lean kernel, not a human referee, checks the proofs.
